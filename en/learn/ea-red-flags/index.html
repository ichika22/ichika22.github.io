7 red flags when buying a forex EA

What sellers show you, what they leave out, and what to ask for instead.

Published Oct 21, 2026 · 6 min read

I develop EAs myself, so I see the marketing side from the inside. These are the patterns that should make you slow down before paying for, or copying, any automated system.

1. A very high win rate with no average loss shown

A 95% win rate sounds safe until you notice that the average loss is ten or twenty times the average win. Many grid and martingale systems win almost every trade and then lose months of profit in one move. Always ask for average win vs average loss and the largest single loss.

2. No maximum drawdown next to the gain

A gain without drawdown is half the information. An 80% drawdown needs a 400% gain to recover (see the table). If a seller shows the gain in large type and hides the drawdown, that is a choice.

3. Screenshots instead of a verified, third-party record

Screenshots and spreadsheets can be edited or cherry-picked. Prefer a live account tracked by a third party such as Myfxbook, with "track record verified" and "trading privileges verified" where available.

4. Only a backtest, or a backtest that looks too perfect

A perfectly smooth equity curve over 20 years often means the system was tuned to past data. A backtest is a starting point. A live record of at least 12 months with hundreds of trades tells you much more.

5. "Guaranteed", "risk-free", or fixed monthly returns

No legitimate trading system can guarantee returns. Fixed promises such as "10% every month" are a classic sign of something that is not what it claims to be.

6. A track record that is too short or restarts often

If accounts are frequently closed and replaced with new ones, the old ones may have blown up. Ask how long the current account has been running and whether earlier accounts exist.

7. No clear disclosure of how the seller is paid

Sellers are often paid by the broker (as an Introducing Broker), by subscription fees, or both. That is normal, but it should be disclosed so you can judge the incentive. For transparency: I am an IB for Tradeview, and my strategies are available only through Tradeview's copy-trading service.

What to ask before you start

  • The live, verified account link, and when it was last updated.
  • Max drawdown (equity, not only balance) and the worst month.
  • Average win, average loss, number of trades, account age.
  • Minimum capital, all fees, and how stopping or cancelling works.
  • How the seller earns money from you.
