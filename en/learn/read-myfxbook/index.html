How to read a Myfxbook account in 2 minutes

Gain, drawdown, verification badges and the numbers sellers hope you skip.

Published Oct 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Myfxbook is the most common place where EA developers and signal providers publish live results. A public page has a lot of numbers. Here is the order I read them in.

1. Is it verified, and is it a real account?

Look for Track Record Verified (Myfxbook received the history directly from the broker) and Trading Privileges Verified (the owner gave an investor password, so the account cannot be edited from the page). Also check that the account type says Real, not Demo.

2. When was it last updated?

A page can stop syncing and still show old, flattering numbers. Check the update time near the top. If an account has not updated for weeks, treat its figures as stale.

3. Gain vs absolute gain

Gain on Myfxbook is time-weighted. It removes the effect of deposits and withdrawals, so it reflects the strategy rather than how much money was added. Absolute gain is simple profit divided by deposits. If the two are very different, money was moved in or out during the period; that is not wrong, but it is worth understanding.

4. Drawdown, the most important number

Read Drawdown right after gain. A big gain with a big drawdown means the ride was rough. Remember that recovery is not linear: a 50% drawdown needs a 100% gain to recover (full table).

5. Trades, pips, and average win vs loss

Open the Stats tab. Check the number of trades (more is better for judging), the average win and average loss, and the profit factor. A high win rate with a much larger average loss is a warning sign.

6. Account age and the monthly table

Scroll to the monthly returns. Look for losing months and how deep they were. A record of a few weeks or months says little; a year or more says much more.

7. Leverage and lot size

High leverage and growing lot sizes after losses (martingale behavior) can produce smooth curves until they do not. Check whether lot sizes stay in a sensible range in the trade history.
