Risk math

Why a 20% loss needs a 25% gain to recover

The drawdown recovery table every EA buyer and copy trader should know.

Published Oct 21, 2026 · 5 min read

Losses and gains are not symmetric. If an account drops 20%, it now has 80% of its starting balance. To get back to 100%, those 80 units must grow by 20 units, which is 25% of 80, not 20%.

The formula

Required gain to recover = loss ÷ (1 − loss).

The deeper the drawdown, the faster the required gain grows:

DrawdownGain needed to get back to the peak
10%11.1%
20%25.0%
30%42.9%
40%66.7%
50%100%
60%150%
70%233%
80%400%
90%900%

What this means when you judge an EA

  • Read the max drawdown before the gain. A system showing +300% with an 80% drawdown went through a period where it needed +400% just to break even again.
  • Your entry date matters. Someone who started near a peak lived through the full drawdown. Someone who started at the bottom saw only the recovery. The same public track record can mean very different results for different followers.
  • Time is part of the cost. Recovering from a deep drawdown can take months. Ask yourself whether you would really keep the EA running through that.

A real example from my own accounts

One of my EAs, LAUREL, is an aggressive system. Its public Myfxbook record shows a large total gain, and also a realized maximum drawdown of about 82%. By the table above, an 82% drawdown requires roughly a 460% gain to get back to the previous peak. I publish that number on purpose: if you only look at the gain, you are not seeing the risk you would actually live through.

You can check the current figures yourself on the public Myfxbook profile. Figures change every week; always check the update date.

Balance drawdown vs equity drawdown

Some reports show drawdown on balance (closed trades only). Others use equity, which includes floating losses on open positions. Grid and martingale-style systems can look calm on balance while equity drops sharply. When you compare systems, make sure you compare the same kind of drawdown.

Practical rules

  1. Decide in advance the maximum drawdown you can tolerate, in money, not just in percent.
  2. Size your account or lot so that the system's historical max drawdown stays within that limit, and assume the future max can be worse.
  3. Never add money only because a system is in a drawdown, unless that was your plan before you started.
Past drawdowns are not a ceiling. A live system can exceed its previous maximum drawdown at any time.
