Copy trading an EA vs buying an EA: what actually changes

Costs, minimums, control and risks, explained with Tradeview's copy-trading service as the example.

Published Oct 21, 2026 · 6 min read

There are two common ways to use someone else's automated strategy: buy the EA and run it yourself, or copy a master account that runs it. I offer my strategies only through copy trading, so here is an honest comparison.

Buying an EA and running it yourself

  • You need MT4/MT5, a VPS that runs 24/5, and correct settings and updates.
  • Pros: full control of lot size, symbols and timing; you can move brokers.
  • Cons: setup errors, VPS downtime, and outdated versions are common reasons why buyers get different results from the seller's account.

Copy trading a master account

  • You need an account with the broker that offers the copy service, and enough capital for the minimum.
  • Pros: no VPS or EA installation; trades are copied from the master account the developer runs.
  • Cons: you depend on the broker's copy system and the master; your fills, start date and costs can differ, so your results will not exactly match the master.

Questions to ask any copy-trading provider

  1. Where is the master account's live, verified record, and how fresh is it?
  2. What is the maximum drawdown so far, and how much capital would that be for you?
  3. What are all the fees: monthly, performance, spread and commission?
  4. What happens to open positions when you stop copying?
  5. How is the provider paid? (I am a Tradeview IB, so I earn commission when followers trade.)
